Why this work matters

School may provide textbooks, but families still pay for many learning essentials.

For families with limited income, the cost of notebooks, stationery, supplementary books, bags, and other supplies can make school harder. We focus on helping with those practical needs.

24% of household spending on secondary education went to books and stationery World Bank analysis of Bangladesh HIES 2016/17. This category includes textbooks, notebooks, exercise books, and stationery.
Basic equipment Students without basic school materials were more likely to attend irregularly or achieve poorly Finding from CREATE’s nationwide community and school survey in Bangladesh.
Free materials A Bangladesh program for disadvantaged children included books, school bags, uniforms, and stipends These materials were one part of the World Bank-supported ROSC II program, alongside learning centers, teacher support, and monitoring.

What we provide

We help students get the school materials they need.

From supplementary textbooks and notebooks to backpacks, pencils, and other basic supplies, we provide practical support that helps students learn with confidence.

How it works

A simple process that puts students first.

Teachers identify students

Teachers and school staff help identify students who need support.

We confirm what is needed

The school tells us which books and supplies will help each student most.

Our local team buys the supplies

Our volunteers purchase the approved materials locally whenever possible.

Students receive their materials

The supplies are delivered, recorded, and confirmed by the student or school.

Our mission

Every student deserves the tools to do their best.

Our goal is simple: help students succeed by providing supplementary books and basic school supplies when their families cannot afford them.

Research and references

Sources used on this page

These sources were selected because they directly address household education costs, access to basic school materials, or programs that provided books and supplies to disadvantaged students in Bangladesh.

  1. World Bank — Bangladesh Education Sector Public Expenditure Review, uses Bangladesh Household Income and Expenditure Survey data to describe what families spend on fees, books and stationery, tutoring, transport, and other education costs.
  2. CREATE / University of Sussex — Poverty, Equity and Access to Education in Bangladesh, links poverty and lack of basic school equipment with irregular attendance, poor achievement, repetition, and other forms of educational exclusion.
  3. World Bank — Giving Bangladesh’s Poor Children a Chance at Education, describes the multi-part ROSC II program for disadvantaged children, including free books, school bags, uniforms, stipends, learning centers, and teacher support.
  4. Asian Development Bank — Improved Teaching Methods Help Secondary Students in Bangladesh Learn Better, shows how poverty-targeted secondary education support helped students purchase practical materials such as books, pencils, and notebooks.
  5. UNCDF — Low Income Lives: Education, a small household diary study showing that low-income families may still face costs for fees, stationery, supplementary textbooks, and tutoring even where government textbooks are provided.
